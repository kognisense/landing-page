The UK's Sustainability Disclosure Requirements (SDR) mark a significant shift in how businesses report environmental and social impacts. For Food SMEs, this isn't just another regulatory checkbox—it's a fundamental change to how you demonstrate value to retailers and customers.

## What is UK SDR 2026?

The SDR, introduced by the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA), requires companies to disclose sustainability-related information following the recommendations of the Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures (TCFD). While initially targeting large corporations, the ripple effect is already impacting the entire supply chain.

## Who's Affected?

### Direct Requirements
- Companies with over 500 employees
- Listed companies on UK exchanges
- Financial institutions managing significant assets

### Indirect Impact (The Supply Chain Ripple)
- **Food SMEs supplying major retailers** - Tesco, Sainsbury's, M&S are already requesting Scope 3 data
- **Companies with 250+ employees or £36M+ turnover** - SECR compliance remains mandatory
- **Export-focused businesses** - EU customers increasingly require sustainability documentation

## Key Reporting Requirements

### 1. Scope 1, 2, and 3 Emissions
Track and report greenhouse gas emissions across:
- **Scope 1**: Direct emissions (company vehicles, on-site fuel combustion)
- **Scope 2**: Indirect emissions (purchased electricity, heating)
- **Scope 3**: Supply chain emissions (the big challenge for most SMEs)

### 2. Climate Risk Assessment
Identify physical and transitional climate risks affecting your business operations.

### 3. Governance and Strategy
Demonstrate how sustainability is integrated into business decision-making.

## The Timeline

- **Q1 2026**: First mandatory SECR reports due for companies with December year-ends
- **Mid-2026**: Major UK retailers enforce supplier Scope 3 data requirements
- **2027**: Extended disclosure requirements for medium-sized enterprises

## Why Manual Compliance Isn't Sustainable

The average Food SME spends **40-60 hours** manually extracting data from invoices, utility bills, and delivery receipts for SECR compliance. With SDR expanding requirements, this burden could triple.

### Common Pain Points:
- Inconsistent invoice formats from multiple suppliers
- Missing emissions data on utility bills
- Manual spreadsheet errors causing audit failures
- Time-consuming data reconciliation across systems
